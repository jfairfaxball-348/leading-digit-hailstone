# Conjecture-Candidate Assessment

Date: 2026-09-26.

Current gate: **CANDIDATE_DISTINCTIVE_CONJECTURE**.

## Simplicity of statement

Strong. The map differs from the familiar hailstone template by one ordinary decimal statistic, and the conjecture is a single universal eventual-entry statement.

## Non-obviousness

Strong enough to justify study. The leading digit is a genuinely nonperiodic state selector: it cannot be reduced to a fixed finite congruence partition, and the odd branch changes discontinuously at decimal boundaries.

## Computational robustness

Strong as finite evidence, not as proof. Exhaustive testing through 5,000,000, targeted boundary tests, a search for long runs of v2=1, and 50/100/200-digit constructed starts all converge to the same observed cycle under their explicit protocols.

## Structural richness

Strong. Exact 2-adic residue classes, decimal-sector affine pieces, boundary jumps, almost-unique odd inverse images, an accelerated-cycle equation, and the non-RCWA result give several independent mathematical handles.

## Distinctiveness from nearby rules

Meaningful but mixed. The frozen rule is the only one-cycle member of the five tested global shifts through 100,000, while 11 of 18 one-coordinate perturbations develop multiple cycles. However, 7 of 18 one-coordinate perturbations also show one observed cycle, so the frozen rule is not uniquely isolated.

## Prior-art risk

Still material. The 7-cycle is definitely old 5x+1 behavior. The deeper audit has not located the full rule exactly or equivalently, and the standard finite-modulus generalized-Collatz class does not contain it, but negative search evidence cannot establish novelty.

## Suitability as a named Collatz-adjacent conjecture

Supported at the present pilot level. “Leading-Digit Hailstone Conjecture” is a useful descriptive project name for the candidate statement. It should not be presented as a historical priority claim until the prior-art audit is stronger.

## Bottom line

The object has cleared the “generic toy rule” concern well enough to continue. The unresolved question is now primarily bibliographic/equivalence risk, not whether there is any mathematical content worth studying.
